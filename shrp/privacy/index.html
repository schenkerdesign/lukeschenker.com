SHRP Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 July 2026. Developer: Luke J. Schenker, operating as Schenker Design, Gibsonia, Pennsylvania, USA. Contact: luke@anew.so.

Beta notice. SHRP is in a small, closed beta. This policy describes how the beta handles your data. It will be updated before any public release.

1. The short version

SHRP helps you understand and reduce the time you spend on social media, and lets people encourage each other along the way. Two things are worth knowing up front:

  • Some of your information is public by design. If you choose a username, a fixed public subset of your data (your username, your daily total social-media screentime, your medal, streak, percentile, your usage trends, and your typical daily patterns) becomes visible to anyone, including people who don't have an account. We explain this in section 4.
  • Your most detailed record stays on your phone. The exact minute-by-minute log of which apps you used and when never leaves your device. Once you claim a username, we sync summaries of your usage to our servers to run the social features and to understand and improve the product. All of it is tied to an anonymous ID, never your name, and we never share or sell it.

4. How your data is shared: the three visibility tiers

SHRP sorts your data into three tiers. Which tier a piece of data is in determines who can see it.

  • Public: anyone can see it, including people without an account. Your username, daily total social-media screentime, medals, streak, global percentile, your usage trend charts, and your typical daily-usage patterns. This public subset is the same for every user, and it's visible on the web to logged-out visitors too. This is a deliberate design choice: SHRP is meant to be browsable and comparable without an account. You only enter the public tier when you claim a username; before that, your app is entirely private to your device.
  • Relationship-gated: only people you follow can see it. Right now this is your display name (real name), if you enter one. Here, following works the opposite way from most social apps: following someone lets them see your relationship-gated data. Following is an act of sharing more of yourself with that person, not a request to see their content. To let someone see your real name, you follow them.
  • Private from other users. Your per-app breakdown and your inbox of received messages are never visible to other users. Your per-app breakdown does sync to our servers, where we use it pseudonymously to understand and improve the product (see section 6) — it is simply never shown to anyone else. Your raw minute-by-minute log stays on your device.

6. How we use your data

  • To provide the app: show your screentime, medals, feeds, and messages, and let people encourage each other.
  • To understand and improve the product: we collect data about how you use SHRP, plus crash diagnostics and any feedback you send, all tied to an anonymous ID and never to your name. It is pseudonymized, and we never share or sell it.
  • To learn from your feedback: read what you submit so we can improve SHRP.
  • We do not use your data for advertising, and we do not sell it.

7. Analytics

Our analytics (PostHog) records pseudonymous events tied to an anonymous identifier — never your name, email, phone, message text, or raw screentime. We use it to understand how the app is used so we can improve it.

8. How long we keep data, and deletion

  • On your device: detailed screentime stays until you delete the app or clear its data.
  • On our servers: we keep your account data while your account exists.
  • Deleting your account. The app has a Delete account control (Settings → Account). When you use it, we permanently delete your account and the data tied to it in our operational database, and the app wipes its local data (your on-device screentime history and app preferences) from your phone. Specifically:
    • Your profile (username, display name, avatar, and any optional fields) and your synced screentime totals and scores are deleted.
    • Your follow relationships are removed.
    • Messages you have sent are deleted, including the copies that appear in other people's inboxes. A preset message you sent is treated as no longer yours once your account is gone, so it is removed rather than reassigned.
    • If you are the host of a group, deleting your account deletes that group, including its membership and invitations, for all of its members. (If you want to keep the group, transfer or wind it down before deleting your account. We expect to make group ownership transferable in a future version.)
  • Feedback you submitted is kept, but de-identified. If you sent us feedback (a Fix / Idea / Anything note, an optional rating, or a screenshot), we unlink it from your account and retain it in our private store so we can keep learning from it to improve SHRP. Once unlinked, it is no longer associated with you.
  • Analytics and crash-reporting records are retained in de-identified form. Our analytics (PostHog) and crash reports (Sentry) were never linked to your name, email, or phone, only to an anonymous identifier. On deletion we stop associating new data with you and clear that identifier on your device; the historical, de-identified records are retained to understand the product in aggregate. Aggregated or anonymized statistics that can no longer identify you may be kept indefinitely.
  • Backups. Deleted data may persist in routine encrypted backups of our database for up to 30 days, after which those backups rotate out.

9. Security

We protect your data with row-level access controls on our database (so each user can only read what they're allowed to), encryption of sensitive data on your device, and secrets kept off the app.

10. Children

SHRP is not directed to children. You must be 18 or older to use the beta. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18; if we learn we have, we will delete it.
